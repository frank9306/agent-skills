# Data Visualization

## Start from the decision

Choose a chart only after naming the comparison or decision it supports:

- trend over time: line or area with restrained fill
- category comparison: ordered bar
- part-to-whole: stacked bar; use pie only for a few obvious parts
- distribution: histogram, box plot, or density
- relationship: scatter plot
- progress toward target: bullet chart or direct value with target

Do not add a chart when a number, sentence, or compact table communicates the answer faster.

## Preserve meaning

- Use consistent scales for comparisons and disclose breaks.
- Reserve semantic colors for stable meanings such as danger or success.
- Use direct labels where practical and keep legends close to the data.
- Format numbers, units, dates, and precision consistently.
- Provide accessible names, summaries, and a table alternative for important data.
- Design empty, partial, loading, stale, and error states.
- Test color-blind distinguishability and do not rely on hue alone.

## Product integration

Match chart typography, spacing, controls, focus behavior, tooltips, and surfaces to the product design system. Avoid turning dashboards into decorative card grids. Lead with the questions and actions users need, not with every available metric.

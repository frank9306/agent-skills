# Article structures

Choose one structure before drafting. Combine structures only when the controlling idea requires it.

## Explanation

Use for concepts, systems, and technical subjects:

1. Concrete phenomenon or question
2. Necessary background
3. Mechanism explained in causal order
4. Practical example
5. Limits, exceptions, or misconceptions
6. Consequence for the reader

## Investigation

Use for disputed phenomena or questions requiring evidence:

1. Observable problem
2. Why the obvious explanation is insufficient
3. Evidence grouped by question, not source
4. Competing explanations
5. Best-supported conclusion and remaining uncertainty

## Case-led analysis

Use when one real case reveals a broader mechanism:

1. Specific scene with only verifiable detail
2. Decision, tension, or anomaly
3. Reconstruction of what happened
4. Mechanism behind the outcome
5. Comparison with another case or baseline
6. General lesson without overgeneralizing

## Argument

Use when defending a judgment:

1. State the consequential question
2. Give the thesis and its boundary
3. Present the strongest supporting reasons
4. Address the strongest counterargument fairly
5. Refine the thesis
6. End with implications, not a slogan

## Selection rules

- Start with a scene only when it supplies evidence, tension, scale, or a concrete mental model.
- Use chronology only when sequence explains causality.
- Organize research around reader questions rather than summarizing sources one by one.
- End when the controlling idea has landed; do not repeat the introduction as a conclusion.

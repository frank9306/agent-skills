# Style and quality

## Drafting rules

- Prefer the exact actor and action over abstract nominalizations.
- Replace broad adjectives with observable evidence.
- Distinguish correlation, mechanism, inference, and speculation.
- Put numbers in context with units, time period, baseline, and source.
- Attribute judgments to their speaker or evidence base.
- Vary sentence length for comprehension, not decoration.
- Use examples that test or clarify the claim, not merely echo it.
- Keep metaphors consistent and discard them once they stop explaining.
- Avoid prefabricated openings, rhetorical questions without payoff, false suspense, and inspirational endings unrelated to the evidence.

## Revision passes

1. **Argument:** Does each section advance the controlling idea?
2. **Evidence:** Can every checkable claim be sourced or clearly qualified?
3. **Precision:** Can vague nouns, verbs, pronouns, or modifiers be made concrete?
4. **Compression:** Remove repeated setup, conclusions, and throat-clearing.
5. **Reader model:** Add only the context the intended reader needs.
6. **Rhythm:** Break overloaded sentences and join choppy fragments where logic belongs together.
7. **Originality:** Remove imitation of a named author's signature phrasing or recurring rhetorical tics.

## Scoring rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 10:

- **Factual integrity:** Claims are traceable, qualified, and internally consistent.
- **Structural clarity:** The order follows reader questions and causal logic.
- **Precision and density:** Sentences carry concrete information without clutter.
- **Narrative momentum:** Examples and transitions create forward movement without artificial suspense.
- **Image-text fit:** Every image explains, evidences, compares, or grounds something specific.

Require at least 42/50. Require factual integrity and image-text fit to reach 9/10. Revise the weakest dimension first.
